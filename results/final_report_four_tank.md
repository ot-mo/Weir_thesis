## Known open issues / lessons learned so far

- For any block whose mean total exceeds about 20 V the per-pump rails force the block-mean split into [T-24, 24-T], so at a 3 V total excess the split may only sit within +/-3 V of zero mean; a large total excess can therefore only be carried by a NEGATIVE split displacement, i.e. every high-total excursion on this plant is directionally tank-1-like by rail geometry alone, and the mirrored tank-2 directional branch can never fire at any magnitude gate above ~1.2 V.
- A split-anchored symmetric (balanced double leak) branch is self-defeating under the rails: requiring |mean(v1)-mean(v2)-3.05| < 1.2 V caps the block-mean total at 2*v1max - 1.85 = 22.15 V, so any symmetric-branch threshold above dT = 4.38 V is unreachable by healthy data but equally unreachable by a balanced double leak - the branch is either unsafe or vacuous, which is the geometric form of the both_leaks unobservability floor.
- The episode-start fill couples the magnitude and the error conditions instead of decoupling them: reaching a block-mean total of 20.7 V from the healthy 17.77 V requires roughly 80 percent of the 100-sample block to be fill, which forces the same block's mean|error| to about 0.18 m against a 0.105 m guard, so the total-excess gate and the error guard cannot be satisfied on the same block - the false-positive class is excluded by construction rather than by tuning.
- An error floor of 0.03 m in a mean|error| gate is inert, not conservative: the healthy steady-state mean|error| is 0.074-0.083 m, so such a term is true at every step and the whole decision is carried by the effort term it was meant to protect; any error-based guard on this plant must sit ABOVE 0.083 m to do any work and below about 0.13 m to preserve steady-state detectability.
- For any fixed-length block-mean detector with a step-like disturbance of steady-state excursion Q and a threshold THR < Q, the onset latency and the post-clear false-flag tail are complements that always sum to exactly the block length B: the detector misses the first B*THR/Q steps of the disturbance and keeps firing for B*(1 - THR/Q) steps after it ends. The total 'contamination' of a fault episode is therefore fixed at B steps no matter how the threshold is tuned; the threshold only decides how that fixed B is split between missed steps and post-clear false steps, so with a missed-step cost (300) higher than a faulted-scenario false-step cost (160) the optimal split biases the threshold DOWN toward the healthy-envelope floor, not up toward the physical ceiling.
- The episode-start symmetric fill traverses a straight line in (total, split) space from the healthy anchor (17.77, 3.05) toward (24, 0), so for any block whose mean total already clears a 3.0 V magnitude gate (T >= 20.77) the fill's shape ratio (split deviation)/(total excess) is at most 3.05/3.0 = 1.017 and falls to 0.49 at the rail. Consequently a shape band whose lower edge is set at 1.05-1.1 rejects the ENTIRE symmetric-fill family by geometry alone, independently of the error channel; the error guard is then a redundant second line of defence rather than the only thing standing between the fill and a false positive, which means the error guard's threshold can be shifted without opening a new false-positive path.
- Because a leak's total excess and its split displacement are both first-order in the fault magnitude and both scale linearly with the faulted fraction of the block, the shape ratio is invariant not only for any leak size but also along the entire rise and fall of the fault, where the block is a mixture of healthy and faulted samples (both deviations are scaled by the same mixture factor). This makes the shape test safe to evaluate on a shorter nested confirmation sub-block at partial saturation, and means only the magnitude gate, not the shape band, carries the detectability-versus-false-positive trade-off.
- A supervisor that compares the most recent half of the 50-sample window against the window's OWN earlier half is structurally blind to a sustained fault (once the fault outlasts the window both halves are equally contaminated and the ratio relaxes back to ~1) while remaining maximally sensitive to the episode-start fill TRANSITION; this reproduces exactly the observed signature of ~50 flags per episode located outside every fault window and zero flags inside any of them, i.e. a detector that is pure false-positive with zero true detections.
- The residual false-positive class that neither a block-shape test nor a half-window persistence test can remove is a sustained anti-phase plateau - a full 50 s with the high-headroom pump pinned at its 12 V rail and the other near its healthy mean (e.g. v2 ~ 10.8-12 V with v1 ~ 10 V, giving dT ~ 3 V and |dS|/dT ~ 1.3-1.6) - because that state satisfies the full-window and half-window tests identically; only the error channel separates it, since a PID can hold a pump at its rail only while that tank is persistently off target by more than the healthy block-mean |error| of 0.074-0.083 m, so the error guard is the load-bearing defence against the one FP mode the shape band cannot see.
- On this plant the two available effort channels carry strictly complementary information so neither is usable alone: the split channel's healthy swing is larger than its own mean (any split-only test is FP-bound), while the total channel's healthy 50-sample mean excursion (~2.5 V) is comparable to the largest fault signature (q <= 3.48 V for tank-1 faults), so the safe detection region is the INTERSECTION of two tails and the achievable false-positive rate is the product of two marginal tail probabilities - which is the only reason a 3.0 V magnitude gate can be used at all.
- The reported combined IAE is exactly 94.698 in all six scenarios (fault-free baseline and every leak/severe case alike), i.e. 600 steps x (0.0740+0.0832); the score is therefore completely insensitive to the supervisor's setpoint output on this battery and the entire gradient lives in the missed_anomaly/false_positive terms, so any edit justified only by 'reducing IAE' is worth exactly zero points here while every edit to the flag logic is worth up to 300 or 250-160 points per step.
- The start-up fill's shape ratio is not a constant but the function 3.05/dT (its split displacement saturates at -3.05 V while its total excess keeps growing toward +6.23 V), so a constant shape band rejects the fill only in one dT slice; the correct construction is a magnitude-dependent shape bound (loose lower edge permitted at high dT where the fill ratio has already decayed, tight band only at low dT where the fill ratio coincides with the leak ratio 1.667) - which is exactly why a 'strong' tier at dT > 2.6 V is fill-immune by geometry while a 'weak' tier at dT ~ 1.8 V is not.
- Because the leak's split-to-total ratio is an exact plant constant (1/(1-2*gamma) = 1.667) that is preserved for every fault magnitude and every partially-faulted block, the shape band can be narrowed to +/-10% of that constant rather than the champion's +/-40%; the recovered selectivity is the only currency that can be spent on lowering the magnitude gate into the region where the healthy total envelope (2.5 V) overlaps the fault signature, so widening the band and lowering the gate together is strictly self-defeating while narrowing the band and lowering the gate is the only monotone improvement available.
- A zero-false-positive supervisor is not evidence of a correct detector on this battery: the champion's 0 FP / 0 TP profile across all six scenarios is indistinguishable from permanently dead code, so any admissible candidate must additionally demonstrate reachability - the weakest arm of the decision tree must be shown to be crossable by at least one fault class under the published rail geometry (for tank 1, dT > 2.0 V is crossable up to the v2-rail cap q <= 3.48 V, whereas for tank 2 no total-excess arm above ~1.2 V is crossable at all, so a symmetric two-tank detector must accept an intentionally asymmetric tank-2 gate).
- The champion's zero-detection profile at gates dT > 3.0 V (full 50 s) AND dTr > 2.4 V (last 25 s) across 649 tank-1 fault-steps bounds the real tank-1 fault signature in the total channel to below roughly 2.4-3.0 V - i.e. close to but below the v2-rail cap of 3.48 V - so combined with the measured healthy block-mean envelope (up to ~20.3 V) the usable total-excess gate is only about 2.0-2.4 V and the false-positive budget must be spent on the shape band and the error guard, never on threshold height.
- Because the nested confirmation statistic covers half as many samples as the primary block, its standard error is sqrt(2) times larger, so an AND-ed confirmation gate set only 0.6 V below the primary gate (2.4 V vs 3.0 V) is the binding reachability constraint on the whole detector: it delays detection onset by ~B*THR/q samples and shortens the post-clear false-flag tail by the same amount, and with a missed-step cost of 300 against a faulted-scenario false-step cost of 160 the confirmation gate should be set at roughly half the primary gate so it buys tail shortening without paying for it in missed detections.
- A tank-2 directional branch is forced by the v1 rail (total excess capped at ~1.19 V) to use a magnitude gate that lies strictly inside the measured healthy block-mean total envelope (~2.5 V), so unlike the tank-1 arm it cannot buy any false-positive safety with threshold height at all and must be gated on the split channel plus the error guard; the asymmetric gate pair (2.2 V tank-1 / 1.0 V tank-2) is therefore dictated by plant geometry rather than being a tuning choice, and any symmetric pair of gates is structurally wrong here.
- A full-window magnitude gate is a hard reachability ceiling, not a latency dial: a disturbance with steady-state excursion Q can never pull a B-sample block mean above Q no matter how long it lasts, and reaching a fraction f of that steady state costs roughly f*B samples PLUS the plant's 100-300 s closed-loop settling, so with the tank-1 signature bounded below ~2.4 V and the tank-2 signature below ~1.2 V by the pump rails, any gate above those values is permanently dead code independent of the shape and error guards - the gate has to be chosen below the smallest excess to be DETECTED, which is a different and much lower bar than 'outside the healthy envelope'.
- A nested confirmation sub-block must have its shape band widened, not just its magnitude gate lowered: the ratio of two block means carries a relative standard error that grows like 1/sqrt(B), so a B/2-sample confirmation of a B-sample shape test has ~1.41x the ratio noise, and reusing the primary band verbatim silently promotes the confirmation conjunct into the binding, unreachable constraint - i.e. it re-creates the dead-code failure mode through the shape term while the magnitude term looks healthy.
- Setpoint trim is pure downside risk on this plant because the combined IAE is set by the controller alone and is identical in every scenario: a 0.02 m/step trim accumulates to 4-5 m over a 200-250-step fault and can only be unwound at the restore rate, so a 0.02/0.03 trim-restore pair needs ~170 post-clear steps to re-settle while a 0.10 m trim cap with a 0.05/step restore reaches nominal in two steps - the restore_gap term can be made structurally zero at zero cost to any detection or false-positive term.
- Because the healthy 50 s block-mean total is a bounded anti-phase oscillation rather than a Gaussian tail, the false-positive cost of lowering the magnitude gate grows super-linearly as the gate approaches the plateau band (dT ~ 2.5-3.0 V): the plateau is only a few tenths of a volt above the useful gate and its shape ratio (1.3-1.6) already overlaps the leak constant 1.667, so the shape band cannot separate it and the ENTIRE false-positive budget must be carried by the error channel - which means the shape band can be widened almost for free to buy detectability, whereas every volt of gate reduction must be paid for out of the error guard's margin over the healthy 0.085 m block-mean envelope.
- The reachable-and-safe detection window is asymmetric between the two tanks by a factor of about 2: a tank-1 fault can lift the 50 s block-mean total effort up to 3.48 V above the anchor (v2 rail), which straddles the healthy envelope of ~2.5 V and leaves a real slice of usable gate, whereas a tank-2 fault is capped at 1.19 V (v1 rail), strictly inside the healthy envelope, so its safe-gate probability is exactly zero; a designer optimising the average score must therefore allocate all remaining sensitivity to the tank-1 arm and accept zero tank-2 detections, because splitting the budget symmetrically converts a guaranteed gain into a baseline false-positive rejection.
- Because the two directed arms are separated by the SIGN of the split displacement and a single-branch fault preserves that sign for every contamination fraction of the block, adding a correctly-signed tank-1 arm cannot create a false positive in a tank-2-only episode (or vice versa); consequently the per-tank 'never-faulted' false-positive penalty (250, the largest FP weight) is unreachable for any directed arm and can only be incurred via an undirected / balanced branch, which makes the undirected branch the ONLY place where multi-scenario FP risk is created.
- The healthy split channel is structurally high-variance rather than low-noise: with per-pump std 3.16 (v2) and 2.27 (v1) the split's healthy per-sample variance is at least (3.16-2.27)^2 = 0.79 V^2 and at most 29.4 V^2, so any detector that gates on the split MAGNITUDE alone inherits that variance directly; only the split-to-total RATIO is fault-invariant and magnitude-independent, which is why the ratio - not the split level - must carry the shape test.
- On this battery the magnitude channel and the shape channel have to be assigned different roles: the magnitude gate only has to clear the fault's own excess (a reachability condition, whose target is the OBSERVED ~2.0-2.4 V signature, not the rail-cap bound of 3.48 V, which is a loose upper bound), while the shape and error channels carry the entire false-positive budget; using the rail cap as the design target for the gate is precisely the error that makes the detector permanently silent.
