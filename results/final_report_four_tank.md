## Known open issues / lessons learned so far

- The leak signature has an invariant SHAPE, not just an invariant sign: solving the two steady-state branch balances when the loop fully rejects the fault gives delta(v1+v2) = q and delta(v1-v2) = -q/(1-2*gamma) for a tank-1 leak (mirror-signed for tank 2), so the split always moves 1/(1-2*gamma) = 1.667x as much as the total, independently of leak size; a detector that requires a simultaneous total excess AND a split displacement of the OPPOSITE sign with magnitude between roughly 0.5x and 5x the total excess is therefore a shape test that stays valid for any fault magnitude yet rejects both pure oscillation modes.
- A detector may safely pair an EXTERNAL location anchor (the measured healthy means 17.77 V total and 3.05 V split, which the telemetry window cannot contaminate) with an INTERNAL scale anchor (the current window's own standard deviation, which a leak barely changes because it shifts the effort distribution rather than widening it); this hybrid survives both the fill transient and the oscillation phase, whereas a fully self-referential baseline is blinded by contamination and a fully fixed absolute threshold is either unreachable or false-positive-prone at this plant's scale.
- The error guard has selective power that a pure silence/persistence filter does not: every healthy false-positive mode that raises effort on BOTH branches at once (episode-start double saturation) necessarily requires both levels to sit far below their targets, i.e. a block-mean |error| of ~0.25 m versus 0.074-0.083 m healthy, whereas a genuine symmetric double leak raises both branch efforts with the error channel untouched; gating on block-mean |error| > 0.115 therefore removes the symmetric false-positive class while preserving the symmetric true-positive class (both_leaks), which is otherwise unreachable by any split-ratio test.
- Episode length is recoverable from the published numbers themselves: combined IAE 94.698 divided by the healthy per-step combined |error| (0.0740 + 0.0832) gives ~600 steps, while fault-active windows are only 199-250 steps, i.e. under half the episode; a detector can therefore afford an 80-sample averaging window and still leave more than half of every fault's active steps detectable, so the binding constraint on detection latency is the fault's duty cycle rather than the supervisor's reaction time.
- When the analysis window is long relative to the fault's rise, requiring the recent half of that window to reproduce the whole-window verdict is almost free in latency: at every step after fault onset the half-window contains a strictly larger faulted fraction than the full window, so the half condition is satisfied no later than the full condition; the persistence filter therefore buys reduced spurious-exceedance probability rather than costing missed steps, and only bites when the analysis window is short compared with the fault duration.
- The both-rails saturation state that counterfeits a leak is eliminated by the shape band alone, independent of the error guard: at v1 = v2 = 12 V (the extreme of the measured healthy pump ranges) the total sits at 24 V (dt = +6.23 V) but the split collapses to 0 (ds = -3.05 V), so |ds|/dt = 0.49 falls below the 0.6 lower edge of the 1.667-centred band, meaning the measured healthy extremes of both pumps cannot jointly satisfy the total-excess and shape conditions in any oscillation phase.
- Because the healthy per-pump standard deviations are known (2.27 V and 3.16 V), the worst-case standard error of the window-mean total effort is bounded by sqrt(2.27^2 + 3.16^2)/sqrt(n_eff) = 3.89/sqrt(n_eff) regardless of the unknown cross-correlation, so with a 10-effective-sample window no correlation structure can force a threshold above 2.2 x 3.89/sqrt(10) ~ 2.7 V; leaks whose equivalent pump-voltage magnitude q (which enters as delta(v1+v2) = q and delta(v1-v2) = -q/(1-2*gamma)) exceeds ~2.7 V are detectable by the total-excess statistic on any correlation structure, which fixes a hard detectability floor for this battery.
- Because a leak shifts the effort distribution rather than widening it, the window's own per-sample total-effort standard deviation stays essentially at its healthy value even when a fault is present, so using that in-window deviation only to scale the threshold (rather than as a baseline mean) is self-calibrating without suffering the contamination blindness that kills mean-vs-own-mean residual detectors.
- The shape band alone does NOT exclude the episode-start fill transient; only the joint-rail extreme does. At v1=v2=12 the split collapses to 0 and |ds|/dt = 3.05/6.23 = 0.49, which falls below the 0.6 lower edge and is rejected by shape, but the intermediate fill state v1=v2~10 V gives dt=2.23 and ds=-3.05, i.e. |ds|/dt = 1.37, which sits squarely INSIDE the leak band and would false-flag a tank-1 leak; the fill must therefore be excluded by the error guard (block-mean |error| ~0.25 m during filling vs 0.074-0.083 m healthy), not by the shape test.
- Because the two cross-coupled pumps move dominantly anti-phase (v1 up <=> v2 down), the split v1-v2 is the high-variance channel (its healthy swings are comparable to its own 3.05 V mean) while the total v1+v2 is the quiet channel (anti-phase cancellation keeps its standard deviation well below the naive sqrt(SD1^2+SD2^2)=3.89 V); accordingly the total must carry the magnitude/threshold test and the split only the direction/shape test, because any per-pump or split-magnitude threshold must be raised above the individual pumps' own 2.27-3.16 V standard deviations and thereby becomes unreachable for the ~1.2-1.4x fault signature.
- Choosing a trailing block length B sets detection latency to roughly B*DT_THR/q steps into a fault (the block mean only reaches (k/B)*q after k faulted samples), so on this 600-step plant with 199-250-step faults the window is a latency/FP dial: B=100 with DT_THR=2.5 V costs ~60-90 missed steps of a real fault but keeps the 100-sample total-effort mean within ~2 standard errors of the healthy anchor, whereas shortening B to react faster trades a linear latency gain for a 1/sqrt(B) loss in baseline FP margin on the one scenario where any FP is fatal.
- A 'split not displaced' test must be absolute, not a ratio: the balanced double leak leaves the split sitting on its healthy anchor (ds ~ 0 V against SPLIT_ANCHOR=3.05), whereas the episode-start fill collapses the split toward zero (ds = -3.05 V) while raising the total; both states give a SMALL |ds|/dt once the total is large, so any ratio-based 'split unchanged' criterion is satisfied by both and needs the error guard, while requiring |split_mean - 3.05| < 1.2 V separates them with no help from any other channel.
- A split-sign localiser has an intrinsic blind spot for unbalanced double faults: for simultaneous leaks q1, q2 the total excess is q1+q2 and the split displacement is 1.667*(q2-q1), so whenever |q2-q1| > 0.7*(q1+q2)/1.667 the both_leaks episode falls into the single-branch (directional) band and only ONE tank is ever flagged, leaving the other tank's full fault window as missed-anomaly cost; only a near-balanced leak (|q2-q1| small relative to q1+q2) can reach the symmetric branch, so both branches must be evaluated on every step and OR-ed rather than treated as a mutually exclusive classification.
- On this plant the usable design window for the total-effort threshold is only about 3.7 V wide: the physical ceiling is 24 V (both pumps at their measured 12 V maxima) while the block-mean noise floor sits at 17.77 + ~2.5 V, so any total-excess threshold must live in (20.3, 24) V; the lower edge is set by the anti-phase coupling that keeps the total quiet, and raising the threshold toward the rail buys false-positive margin only by giving up the entire sub-3 V fault class, which is why FP-safety and detectability are simultaneously marginal here rather than independently tunable.
- The champion's whole 64.9k deficit is missed_anomaly cost with exactly zero false positives, and the baseline false-positive tolerance is effectively zero while faulted scenarios keep a ~15%/100-point allowance, so the admissibility test for a new detector runs the opposite way round from the usual one: every gate must be individually UNREACHABLE by the measured healthy envelope (block-mean total <= 20.3 V, block-mean |error| <= 0.085 m) before it is worth adding, because a joint gate set that the healthy data can only breach with all-and'd conditions is still fatal on the one scenario where any single flag costs the whole candidate.
- The two flag paths needed to cover single-branch and balanced-double leaks are mutually exclusive by construction as long as the leak shape is trusted: a single-branch leak produces |split deviation| = 1.667 x total excess, which exceeds any 'split undisplaced' tolerance (<= 1.0 V) the moment the total excess clears a >= 3 V magnitude gate, so an undirected branch can only add flags in episodes where no single branch dominates - this bounds the cost of adding it to the false-positive risk of the never-faulted baseline and of the near-balanced double-leak episode alone, and makes the undirected bar the only free parameter that trades both_leaks coverage against baseline safety.
- A guard derived from the fault-invariant error channel is strictly cheaper than the equivalent tightening of the magnitude threshold: because a genuine fault leaves block-mean |error| at the healthy 0.074-0.083 m level, raising the error-guard bar can only ever exclude the start-up fill state (~0.25 m) and can never exclude a real detection, whereas every extra volt added to the total-excess threshold permanently deletes an entire band of leak magnitudes from detectability - so the budget for false-positive safety should be spent on guards first and on threshold height only afterwards.
- The 12 V per-pump rail caps the leak signature asymmetrically and hard: solving the compensated branch balances with delta(v1+v2)=q and delta(v1-v2)=-/+1.667q gives tank-1 v2=7.36+1.333q<=12 -> q<=3.48 V and tank-2 v1=10.41+1.333q<=12 -> q<=1.19 V, so the total-excess statistic has a physical ceiling of 3.48 V for tank-1 faults and 1.19 V for tank-2 faults against a healthy block-mean noise floor of about 2.5 V; the whole usable threshold window is under 1 V wide and any tank-2 total-excess detection is geometrically impossible.
- For any block whose mean total exceeds about 20 V the per-pump rails force the block-mean split into [T-24, 24-T], so at a 3 V total excess the split may only sit within +/-3 V of zero mean; a large total excess can therefore only be carried by a NEGATIVE split displacement, i.e. every high-total excursion on this plant is directionally tank-1-like by rail geometry alone, and the mirrored tank-2 directional branch can never fire at any magnitude gate above ~1.2 V.
- A split-anchored symmetric (balanced double leak) branch is self-defeating under the rails: requiring |mean(v1)-mean(v2)-3.05| < 1.2 V caps the block-mean total at 2*v1max - 1.85 = 22.15 V, so any symmetric-branch threshold above dT = 4.38 V is unreachable by healthy data but equally unreachable by a balanced double leak - the branch is either unsafe or vacuous, which is the geometric form of the both_leaks unobservability floor.
- The episode-start fill couples the magnitude and the error conditions instead of decoupling them: reaching a block-mean total of 20.7 V from the healthy 17.77 V requires roughly 80 percent of the 100-sample block to be fill, which forces the same block's mean|error| to about 0.18 m against a 0.105 m guard, so the total-excess gate and the error guard cannot be satisfied on the same block - the false-positive class is excluded by construction rather than by tuning.
- An error floor of 0.03 m in a mean|error| gate is inert, not conservative: the healthy steady-state mean|error| is 0.074-0.083 m, so such a term is true at every step and the whole decision is carried by the effort term it was meant to protect; any error-based guard on this plant must sit ABOVE 0.083 m to do any work and below about 0.13 m to preserve steady-state detectability.
- For any fixed-length block-mean detector with a step-like disturbance of steady-state excursion Q and a threshold THR < Q, the onset latency and the post-clear false-flag tail are complements that always sum to exactly the block length B: the detector misses the first B*THR/Q steps of the disturbance and keeps firing for B*(1 - THR/Q) steps after it ends. The total 'contamination' of a fault episode is therefore fixed at B steps no matter how the threshold is tuned; the threshold only decides how that fixed B is split between missed steps and post-clear false steps, so with a missed-step cost (300) higher than a faulted-scenario false-step cost (160) the optimal split biases the threshold DOWN toward the healthy-envelope floor, not up toward the physical ceiling.
- The episode-start symmetric fill traverses a straight line in (total, split) space from the healthy anchor (17.77, 3.05) toward (24, 0), so for any block whose mean total already clears a 3.0 V magnitude gate (T >= 20.77) the fill's shape ratio (split deviation)/(total excess) is at most 3.05/3.0 = 1.017 and falls to 0.49 at the rail. Consequently a shape band whose lower edge is set at 1.05-1.1 rejects the ENTIRE symmetric-fill family by geometry alone, independently of the error channel; the error guard is then a redundant second line of defence rather than the only thing standing between the fill and a false positive, which means the error guard's threshold can be shifted without opening a new false-positive path.
- Because a leak's total excess and its split displacement are both first-order in the fault magnitude and both scale linearly with the faulted fraction of the block, the shape ratio is invariant not only for any leak size but also along the entire rise and fall of the fault, where the block is a mixture of healthy and faulted samples (both deviations are scaled by the same mixture factor). This makes the shape test safe to evaluate on a shorter nested confirmation sub-block at partial saturation, and means only the magnitude gate, not the shape band, carries the detectability-versus-false-positive trade-off.
