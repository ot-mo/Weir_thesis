## Known open issues / lessons learned so far

- Absolute effort thresholds generalize badly because each tank's healthy pump effort scales with its own setpoint: the same fault magnitude shows up as different absolute efforts across scenarios with different targets, so every detection and clearance bar should be expressed as ref + max(absolute_floor, relative_fraction * ref) where ref is that tank's own early-window baseline, not as a global constant.
- When the supervisor's own setpoint depression lowers the faulted tank's pump effort, a clearance test based on recent effort alone can pass while the fault is still active; treating 'this tank's setpoint is below nominal' as a per-call latch that holds the anomaly flag and restores the setpoint in one verification step removes the resulting off/on toggling without needing persistent state.
- Because the pump effort needed to hold a level scales with that level, a detector that compares recent effort against an unscaled baseline will see the fault shrink whenever the supervisor depresses the faulted tank's setpoint; the result is a self-induced detect -> depress -> clear -> restore oscillation that shows up as matched bursts of missed anomalies while the fault is active and false positives just after it clears, and it is removed by scaling the expected healthy effort by (active setpoint / nominal target) rather than by moving the threshold.
- A clearance test that compares the recent effort against the same baseline used to declare the anomaly cancels the detector whenever that baseline is itself fault-contaminated (fault occupying most of the telemetry window), because 'recent approximately equals baseline' then reads as 'recovered'; any parallel absolute or relative cap applied through such a gate is silently nullified, which is why severe faults that start at the beginning of the window are missed for their whole duration.
- Equal, repeated failure counts on the same tank across otherwise different scenarios (e.g. exactly 11 missed / 21 false positives on tank1 in tank1_fault_only and on tank2 in tank2_fault_only) are the fingerprint of a detection statistic that is perturbed by the controller's own action, not of a badly chosen threshold; the fix is to make the statistic invariant to setpoint moves instead of re-tuning the threshold.
- Downstream-on-upstream suppression must be expressed relative to the upstream tank's own current elevation: an absolute downstream bar can never fire at a scenario's operating point (so a simultaneous downstream fault is written off as coupling), while a purely persistence-based bar can misfire on the persistent level sag that the supervisor's own upstream setpoint depression injects into the downstream tank; requiring both a long-window persistent elevation and an elevation larger than upstream_elevation*coupling_gain + margin is what separates the two.
- When a tank's false-positive tolerance is zero (a tank that never faults in a scenario), the dominant FP risk is a latch/clearance branch that can trip on noisy recovery statistics while the setpoint is at nominal; conditioning the latch only on the setpoint actually being depressed (a per-call proxy for a prior flag) and forcing a clear at nominal setpoint removes that risk, because a healthy tank at nominal always reads clear.
- Scaling every detection AND clearance statistic (not just one of them) by nominal_target/active_setpoint is what breaks the self-induced detect->depress->clear->restore cycle; scaling only the detection bar still lets the clearance test see the depressed effort as 'recovered' and re-introduces the matched missed/false-positive bursts.
- Deriving the healthy effort reference as a low quantile of short-window rolling medians across the whole window (rather than a single early-window or full-window quantile) keeps the baseline anchored to the lowest sustained operating level, which survives faults that begin after a short healthy prefix or clear before the window ends, whereas a single early or full quantile is contaminated by faults that persist.
- A cascade suppression rule expressed as an absolute or global downstream bar writes off a genuine simultaneous downstream fault whenever the upstream elevation is large; expressing it as a residual (downstream_excess - gain*upstream_excess) with a strong-signal escape keeps upstream-only coupling suppressed while still flagging coincident faults, because the residual grows with the downstream fault even when the upstream excess is large.
- Normalizing pump effort by the tank's own measured level (effort * nominal_target / level) rather than by the commanded setpoint yields a per-sample setpoint-invariant signal that needs no history of past setpoints, and it self-amplifies genuine level sags; because the effort required to hold a level scales with that level, a healthy tank's normalized effort stays flat no matter where the supervisor has pushed its setpoint, which is what stops the supervisor's own depression from clearing a still-active flag.
- A downstream tank's cascade excursion is driven by (and therefore bounded by) the upstream excursion, so when a genuine simultaneous downstream fault has a similar magnitude to the upstream fault, no downstream-minus-gain*upstream residual test can separate them; the only cue that survives is independent evidence such as a nominal-relative absolute effort ceiling, which is why cascade suppression must be overridden by an absolute ceiling and not only by relative/persistence criteria.
- Because the required pump effort drops with the level, a clearance test anchored to a nominal-relative ceiling (rather than to the same in-window baseline that declared the anomaly) is the only form of recovery check that stays valid when the action that the detector itself caused - setpoint depression - is what lowered the raw effort.
- A blanket cascade rule of the form 'upstream elevated AND downstream raw-effort elevated' suppresses a genuine simultaneous downstream fault for the fault's entire duration, producing a downstream missed-anomaly count on the order of the fault length (e.g. ~90), which is an order of magnitude larger than the oscillation-driven counts; the suppression must therefore be gated by downstream PERSISTENCE and by a residual that compares downstream excess against a fraction of upstream excess, not merely by the downstream signal being above its own threshold.
- A recovery/clearance test that shares its numeric baseline with the detection test produces equal failure counts on any tank whenever a fault fills most of the telemetry window (e.g. identical ~31 missed on tank1 and tank2 severe scenarios), which is the fingerprint of the detector cancelling itself rather than of a badly chosen threshold; an independent anchor (an absolute or nominal-relative effort ceiling) is required for the clearance decision.
- Expressing the healthy effort reference as a low quantile of short-window ROLLING MEDIANS (rather than a single early- or full-window quantile) keeps the reference at the lowest sustained operating level even when the fault onset is inside the window, so the detector stays armed until the window actually contains healthy samples again.
- Using the per-call depressed-setpoint condition as a latch proxy (keep the flag asserted whenever the commanded setpoint is below nominal, and restore it to nominal in one verification pulse) lets a stateless supervisor survive its own setpoint depression, but it is only safe when the pulse's release test is anchored to the absolute ceiling, otherwise the pulse can release the latch while the fault is still active.
- Normalising pump effort by nominal_target/active_setpoint (a per-sample setpoint-invariant effort) and then using that single normalised signal for BOTH the detection and the clearance test removes the detect->depress->clear->restore oscillation: the champion's matched 11 missed / 21 false-positive bursts on a tank both show up exactly when detection uses a setpoint-independent statistic while clearance still compares raw (depressed) effort against the early baseline.
- A blanket cascade rule that suppresses the downstream flag whenever upstream is elevated AND downstream raw effort is elevated will write off a genuine simultaneous downstream fault for the fault's whole duration (order of the fault length, e.g. ~90 samples), which is an order of magnitude worse than oscillation-driven counts; the suppression must be a residual gate (downstream excess must exceed coupling_gain*upstream_excess + margin) OR-ed with a long-window persistence test, so transient upstream coupling (which is bounded by, and dies with, the upstream excursion) is suppressed while a persistent independent downstream elevation is flagged.
- The two downstream failure modes have different count signatures that identify their cause: an oscillation makes a fixed small number of matched missed/false-positive samples on the faulted tank in every single-fault scenario (here 11/21 regardless of tank or noise), whereas an over-aggressive cascade gate makes the downstream miss-count scale with the SIMULTANEOUS fault's duration (~90). A large, contiguous downstream miss-block should therefore be routed to the coupling/suppression logic, never to the threshold.
- The remedy policy and the cascade logic are coupled and cannot be tuned independently: depressing the faulted upstream setpoint lowers the upstream level, which lowers the gravity outflow into the downstream tank, which forces a REAL, persistent rise in the downstream tank's own effort. So the supervisor's own remedy manufactures exactly the persistent downstream signature that a persistence-based cascade test is supposed to interpret as an independent fault, and only a bound relative to the (causing) upstream excess can separate them.
- A clearance test that re-reads the same window-derived baseline the detector used can also be defeated by an ABSOLUTE ceiling, but only if that ceiling gates the clearance as well: a ceiling that merely adds one more OR-term to the detection is still nullified, because the contaminated baseline keeps the recovery test true and the flag clears just as before.
- Once detection and clearance share a single level-normalised effort statistic, the 'setpoint is depressed => hold the flag' latch is unnecessary: the latch only ever compensated for a statistic that leaked setpoint information, so removing the leak removes the need for the workaround and eliminates the restore pulse that the latch otherwise required.
- A cascade gate whose residual is purely proportional to the upstream excess must carry an escape clause keyed to the downstream tank's OWN detection threshold; without it, a large upstream excess makes the proportional bound exceed the downstream signal even when the downstream tank has a genuine independent fault of similar magnitude, which is what produces a ~90-sample contiguous downstream miss block in the both-faults scenario.
- Small matched miss/false-positive bursts (~11/21) in single-fault scenarios and large matched bursts (~31/31) in severe-persistent scenarios are the same defect at different durations rather than two different bugs: the miss count scales with the number of detect->depress->clear->restore cycles, so one statistic-level fix should reduce both, and adding a second absolute threshold to attack only the large one will leave the small one untouched.
