\chapter{Method}
\label{ch:method}
This chapter describes the method as it is implemented: the architecture, the
interface between the generated code and the process, the checks around that
code, how candidates are scored and promoted, the training loop, the language
models, the comparators and the pre-registered protocol for the comparison.
The last section describes the industrial case, the grinding circuit. Parts
of the method were developed on the test beds of Chapters~\ref{ch:toy}
and~\ref{ch:coord}; where a part was added in response to a failure, that
chapter tells why.

\section{Three-layer architecture}
The framework separates real-time execution from high-level reasoning in
three layers (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).
\begin{description}
\item[Low-level control and execution layer] The existing deterministic PID
  loops track the active setpoints at a sub-second rate and are never
  generated or modified by the LLM.
\item[Executable heuristic supervisor] A deterministic Python function that
  runs locally at a slower rate: every 60~s in the grinding circuit and every
  3--10~s on the tank test beds. It reads a window of recent telemetry and
  updates the setpoints; in the fault-detection test beds it also raises one
  anomaly flag per loop.
\item[LLM meta-supervisor] Runs offline. It reads logs of the supervisor's
  performance in simulation and synthesizes new code for the supervisor. It
  is the only layer that calls the LLM's API.
\end{description}
The LLM's cost, latency and non-determinism therefore never affect the
real-time loop. Before generated code is loaded into the executable
supervisor, it must pass the security check in Section~\ref{sec:sandbox} and
improve on the current supervisor in the evaluation of
Section~\ref{sec:scoring}. Between the supervisor and the PID loops sits a
fixed safety wrapper (Section~\ref{sec:wrapper}). Together, the two upper
layers take the place of the TD-MPC supervisory controller.

\section{Supervisor interface}
\label{sec:interface}
Every supervisor is a single Python function that is called at a fixed
decision interval. It is stateless: it is reloaded for every episode, keeps
nothing between calls and sees only its arguments, so its behaviour is fully
determined by the telemetry it is given. Two variants are used.
\begin{description}
\item[Fault detection] On the leak test beds of Chapter~\ref{ch:toy}, the
  function is
  \begin{center}
  \texttt{supervise(telemetry\_window, active\_setpoints, nominal\_targets)}.
  \end{center}
  The telemetry window holds, for each controlled tank
  and each sample, the level, the output of the loop regulating it (pump
  effort) and the control error. The function returns a free-text diagnosis,
  one adjusted setpoint per loop and one boolean anomaly flag per loop. The
  returned flags are held until the next decision.
\item[Setpoint coordination] On the coordination test bed of
  Chapter~\ref{ch:coord} and on the grinding circuit, the function is
  \begin{center}
  \texttt{supervise(telemetry\_window, active\_setpoints, objectives)}
  \end{center}
  and returns a diagnosis and the new setpoints, without anomaly flags. The
  window holds every logged signal together with the setpoints and targets
  that were in force at each sample, as a distributed control system logs
  them, so that the supervisor can see its own earlier moves. The objectives
  hold the targets, the constraint bands and the setpoint limits.
\end{description}

\section{Security sandbox}
\label{sec:sandbox}
Generated code is treated as untrusted and passes three checks before it runs
on any telemetry:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a static check of the abstract syntax tree that rejects imports,
  \texttt{exec}, \texttt{eval}, file and network access, access to dunder
  attributes, sources over 20\,000 characters and anything other than a
  single top-level \texttt{supervise} function with the required signature;
\item execution in a namespace with an explicit whitelist of built-in
  functions and the \texttt{math} and \texttt{statistics} modules, instead of
  Python's real built-ins;
\item a wall-clock timeout on every call (0.5~s), so that an infinite loop
  cannot stall the simulation. On the grinding circuit, a thread that
  overruns is also stopped, because a thread left spinning holds Python's
  global interpreter lock and slows the simulator to a crawl.
\end{enumerate}
An exception or timeout during evaluation is recorded as a failure and the
setpoints are held; in the fault-detection variant both anomaly flags are
also raised. The same sandbox is used on every test bed and on the grinding
circuit.

\section{Safety wrapper}
\label{sec:wrapper}
In the coordination test bed and the grinding circuit, a fixed, human-written
wrapper sits between every supervisor and the loops, generated or not, so
that safety does not depend on code the LLM wrote. A later generation could
otherwise delete its own rate limit, and an early version clamped a NaN
setpoint straight to the lower limit. The wrapper
\begin{itemize}
\item does not call the supervisor, and holds the setpoints, when the latest
  telemetry sample contains a non-finite value;
\item holds a setpoint that is missing or non-finite in the supervisor's
  output, and counts the call as an exception;
\item clamps every setpoint to its limits and lets it move at most a fixed
  step per decision (and rounds integer setpoints such as the number of
  cyclones).
\end{itemize}
Every intervention is logged and counted in the metrics. The rate limit is a
safety net, not a performance limit: it is chosen so that it does not bind in
normal operation.

\section{Evaluation and scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}
Each candidate is simulated in closed loop on fixed, seeded batteries of
scenarios, and each scenario $s$ gets a score $J_s$ that is a weighted sum of
penalties. The battery score $J$ is the average of $J_s$ over the scenarios;
lower is better.

\subsection{Fault detection score}
On the leak test beds the scenarios are a fault-free run, single leaks of
different onset, size and duration, simultaneous leaks, persistent severe
leaks and, for the single- and two-tank processes, sensor noise. The score is
\begin{equation}
  J_s = \mathrm{IAE}_s + 500\,V_s + 300\,M_s + c_{\mathrm{FP}}\,F_s
        + 10^4\,E_s + 200\,R_s ,
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{IAE}_s$ is the integrated absolute tracking error of all
loops, $V_s$ the number of samples outside the safety bounds, $M_s$ and $F_s$
the number of samples, summed over loops, in which a held anomaly flag
disagrees with the true fault state (missed anomalies and false positives),
$E_s$ the number of exceptions and timeouts, and $R_s$ the distance between
the final and the nominal setpoints for loops whose fault has cleared. A
false positive on a loop that never faults in the scenario costs
$c_{\mathrm{FP}} = 800$ (250 on the quadruple-tank process); otherwise
$c_{\mathrm{FP}} = 160$. Without this asymmetry, flagging constantly was a
cheap way to avoid missed anomalies. For the quadruple-tank process an
\emph{oracle} is also evaluated. It sets each flag to the true fault state at
every decision and keeps the nominal setpoints, which is the best detection
possible at the given decision interval, so that a plateau caused by the
search can be told from one caused by the test bed.

\subsection{Setpoint coordination score}
For setpoint coordination, the score has one term per objective of the
research questions: the integrated absolute error of the product variable
(production rate or P80), the seconds with the level outside its band and
with the circulating load (or its analog) outside its limits, the seconds
outside the safety bounds, the total variation of the setpoints, and a large
penalty per exception or timeout. The weights of the quadruple-tank version
are given in Equation~\eqref{eq:coord-score} and those of the grinding
circuit in Section~\ref{sec:mill}. The recovery time after each disturbance
or target change, the time until the product variable settles within a
tolerance of its target for good, is reported but not scored.

\subsection{Batteries}
\label{sec:batteries}
The scenarios cross the disturbance kind (three channels, and a combination
of two of them at three quarters of the amplitude) with the temporal pattern
(step, ramp or sine), plus nominal scenarios in which only the target changes.
Every cell is drawn from a seeded random generator, with several scenarios per
cell. The batteries have different roles:
\begin{description}
\item[Development] Disturbance amplitudes inside the development range.
  It drives promotion, and the LLM sees the champion's per-scenario results
  and decision traces on it.
\item[Held-out] The same scenario types and ranges with another seed. It is
  never used for promotion, and its score is shown to the LLM only as the
  gap to the development score, as a warning against fitting the visible
  scenarios.
\item[Beyond] Amplitudes beyond the development range, never shown to the
  LLM and only reported. It tests RQ2 on a frozen policy.
\item[Sealed test] A battery that is generated once, fingerprinted and opened
  only in the final evaluation of the protocol (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}),
  with every access logged.
\end{description}
Because the held-out gap is part of the prompt, the held-out battery is not a
clean test; only the sealed battery is.

\subsection{Promotion}
A candidate is promoted only if it improves the development score \emph{and}
passes a regression guard. On the leak test beds the guard is per scenario: no
single scenario may get worse than the champion by more than 5 points (the
fault-free scenario) or the larger of 100 points and 15\% (fault scenarios).
Averaging otherwise let a candidate win by breaking one scenario badly. For
setpoint coordination this guard rejected almost every candidate
(Section~\ref{sec:coord-loop}), and it was replaced by a guard per
disturbance type with a schedule. The mean score of each disturbance type may
get worse by at most the larger of 10 points and a relative tolerance, and no
single scenario by more than the larger of an absolute and a relative cap.
The tolerances are loose in generations 1--2 (25\%, cap 300 points or 150\%),
tighter in generations 3--4 (15\%, cap 200 points or 100\%) and tightest from
generation 5 on (10\%, cap 100 points or 50\%). Promotion is elitist: the
champion is replaced only by a better candidate, so the development score of
the champion never increases.

\section{Training loop}
\label{sec:training-loop}
A run starts from a seed supervisor, normally the fixed operating recipe, or
from another run's champion. Each generation of the LLM meta-supervisor
performs the following steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Build a prompt.} The part that is the same in every call comes
  first, so that the provider's prefix cache can reuse it. It describes the
  process at the level a plant engineer would know it (layout, mass balances,
  parameters, loop pairing and tuning, operating point and disturbance
  types), measured statistics of normal operation, the calling contract and
  the safety wrapper, the exact list of allowed built-in functions, the
  scoring rules and the promotion guard, the task and the output format. The
  part that changes contains the champion's source code, its per-scenario
  results and held-out gap, one line per decision for its two worst-scoring
  scenarios, the previous generation's non-promoted candidates with the
  decision trace of the scenario each lost most on, a measured record of the
  changes already tried, and a list of lessons learned.
\item \emph{Sample $K$ candidates} from the LLM in parallel, with the same
  prompt. Each response is a JSON object with a failure analysis, a numeric
  self-check of the proposed change, the new code and at most two new lessons
  of at most 30 words each. In setpoint coordination each candidate must make
  \emph{one targeted change} to the champion; the share of the champion's
  lines it keeps is logged.
\item \emph{Check, load and score} every candidate on the development
  battery. A candidate that fails the security check or cannot be loaded is
  rejected without simulation. The best candidate is promoted if it passes
  the guard, and the new champion is then scored on the held-out battery.
\item \emph{Update the memory of the run.} Lessons are kept only from
  promoted candidates, at most twelve. Every other candidate is recorded by
  the trainer, not by the LLM, as one measured line: its proposed change, its
  score against the champion and the scenarios it gained and lost most on.
  The prompt lists these lines (the last 30) with the rule not to resubmit a
  change that was not promoted. A run seeded with another run's champion
  inherits that run's record.
\end{enumerate}
The loop was built up incrementally. The single- and two-tank experiments
used an earlier version with one candidate per trial ($K=1$), aggregate
failure counts instead of decision traces and only a short qualitative
description of the process, and they were run while the setup was still
changing, with more than one DeepSeek model. The quadruple-tank fault
experiments used $K=4$, decision traces and lessons written by the LLM. The
guard schedule, the failure traces of rejected candidates, the one-change rule
and the measured record were added in the coordination runs, and $K$ was
lowered to 3 to reduce the cost per generation. On the quadruple-tank fault
task the prompt is about 6\,300 tokens; on the coordination task it grows from
about 17\,000 to 25\,000 tokens over a run, mostly from the traces and the
record.

Every run has its own folders for its candidates, its champion and its logs:
one line per candidate with its decision, score, the model's analysis and
change, and token usage; the per-scenario metrics of every evaluated program;
the lessons; and the settings and code version of every start. A run can be
continued under its name and stopped cleanly between generations. An example
of a generated supervisor is given in Appendix~\ref{app:supervisor}.

\section{Language models}
\label{sec:models}
All runs reported in this thesis used DeepSeek's \texttt{deepseek-flash}
through its API, which on early tests was better than the larger DeepSeek
model on these tasks and is priced at US\$0.15 per million input tokens and
US\$0.60 per million output tokens. Its reasoning effort is configurable:
no thinking, low or high. Output tokens, mostly reasoning, make up about 97\%
of the cost. The trainers also support OpenAI's \texttt{gpt-6-luna} and
\texttt{gpt-6.1-sol}, and the grinding-circuit trainer supports Anthropic's
Claude Opus, Sonnet and Haiku 5.5 with adaptive thinking and a configurable
effort. Weir approved sending plant data and code to Anthropic in October 2026;
for the other external providers this is still open. Each provider has its own
token prices in the trainer, so the cost of every request is logged.

\section{Comparators}
\label{sec:comparators}
Each supervisor is compared with baselines on the same batteries.
\begin{description}
\item[Fixed recipe] The seed supervisor: it holds the nominal operating
  recipe (or rescales it when the target changes) and ignores disturbances.
  It shows what the regulatory loops achieve on their own.
\item[Model predictive controller] For the quadruple-tank coordination test
  bed, a model predictive supervisor written for this thesis, with an
  internal model of the plant and the PI loops and an online disturbance
  estimate. It is evaluated untuned and tuned on the development battery with
  a search budget comparable to the LLM's number of candidates
  (Section~\ref{sec:coord-baselines}).
\item[TD-MPC] For the grinding circuit, Weir's TD-MPC controller
  \cite{hansen2022tdmpc}, trained on the same simulator and acting on the
  same setpoints (Section~\ref{sec:mill}).
\end{description}

\section{Pre-registered protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
During the exploratory coordination runs, the training loop was redesigned
about a dozen times while the development, held-out and beyond results were
being looked at, and the best champion came out of a hand-steered chain of
runs. Such a result cannot test a hypothesis. Before any confirmatory run,
a protocol was therefore registered in the repository. It fixes, in one
commit, the test bed, the score weights, the batteries, the method, the
number of runs, the comparator and the statistics. Any change creates a new
version and restarts the runs; runs under an earlier version are reported,
not discarded. Version~2, the current one, was registered after the PI loops
of the test bed were retuned and the MPC re-tuned accordingly, before any
protocol run and before any access to the sealed battery.

\paragraph{Runs and policies.} $N = 8$ independent runs of six generations
each, all from the fixed recipe, with \texttt{deepseek-flash} at high
reasoning effort and three candidates per generation. A run that fails for
technical reasons is resumed under the same name, never replaced or dropped.
The policy of each run is its final champion; it is not chosen by any other
score.

\paragraph{Comparator.} The primary comparator is the tuned MPC with frozen
parameters; the untuned MPC is secondary, and the fixed recipe and the tuned
MPC with known disturbance are references.

\paragraph{Sealed test battery.} 183 scenarios with their own seed: 42 in the
development range (including nominal target changes), 36 each at 1.25, 1.5
and 2 times a development draw, 24 with sine periods outside the development
range (100--180~s and 550--800~s), and 9 with feed, pump and split
disturbances together. The battery is evaluated once, after all runs are
complete.

\paragraph{Analyses.} All intervals come from a hierarchical bootstrap
($B = 10\,000$) that resamples policies and then scenarios.
\begin{itemize}
\item $H_{0,1}$: the mean paired difference in score, LLM minus tuned MPC,
  over the 8 policies and the 42 development-range test scenarios. $H_{0,1}$
  is rejected if the 95\% interval excludes zero. Failing to reject does not
  establish parity: the two are called equivalent only if the 90\% interval
  lies within $\pm 10\%$ of the MPC's mean score (two one-sided tests).
\item $H_{0,2}$: the slope of the mean $\log(1 + J_s)$ against the amplitude
  level (1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2), LLM minus MPC. $H_{0,2}$ is rejected if the
  95\% interval of the slope difference excludes zero.
\item C3: the median, interquartile range and range of the per-policy mean
  scores, the between-policy standard deviation with a chi-square
  confidence interval, and the number of runs without any promotion.
\end{itemize}
Secondary analyses are the per-term differences with Holm-corrected
bootstrap $p$-values, the score ratios on the unseen periods and
combinations, and results per disturbance type and pattern. Stress tests
with noise, plant mismatch, analyser delay and telemetry faults, and two
ablations (a prompt without the plant equations, and the best of 18 one-shot
samples without the feedback loop) are planned as exploratory.

\paragraph{Simulator.} The industrial case uses Weir's dynamic simulator of a
secondary ball-mill circuit in reverse configuration, written in JAX. Fresh
slurry enters the sump, a pump feeds the sump slurry to a cluster of
hydrocyclones, the cyclone overflow is the product, whose particle size P80 is
the quality variable, and the underflow returns through the ball mill to the
sump. Water is added at the sump and at the mill inlet. The circulating load
is the ratio of underflow solids to fresh-feed solids. The experiment uses the
setting of Weir's TD-MPC environment: explicit Euler integration with a 10~s
scan. One simulated hour takes about 82~s of wall time, most of it in the
regulatory layer, so the batteries are evaluated in a pool of processes.
Episodes last two hours and start from a warm start, the circuit settled for
three hours at the nominal recipe.

\paragraph{Regulatory layer.} The lowest layer is Weir's own controller chain
for the circuit, used unchanged: a feed generator, through which the
disturbances enter, and five PID loops, all sampled every second. The cyclone
pressure is controlled by the pump flow setpoint, with an override on the
sump level near the edges of its range; the pump flow by the pump speed; the
sump density by the setpoint of the sump water flow, which has its own inner
flow loop; and the mill water flow by its valve. The number of operating
cyclones and the mill speed are applied directly.

\paragraph{Setpoints and interface.} The supervisor sets the same five
setpoints as the TD-MPC, within the same limits: the number of operating
cyclones (7--9), the cyclone pressure setpoint (9--13~psi), the sump density
setpoint (1.60--1.80~t/m$^3$), the mill water addition (30--120~t/h) and the
mill speed setpoint (10--15~rpm). The wrapper limits each move to one cyclone,
1~psi, 0.05~t/m$^3$, 20~t/h and 1~rpm per decision (provisional). The
supervisor is called every 60~s, the TD-MPC's action interval, with a window
of the last 30 minutes sampled every 10~s. It sees the TD-MPC's 30 sensors
(fresh feed, mill, sump, pump and cyclone measurements and the circulating
load) and may ask for up to eight more simulator signals. Measurements are
noise-free. The objectives are the P80 target (190~$\mu$m nominally), the sump
level band 70--80\%, the safety bounds of the sump level, 20 and 95\%, at
which the simulator stops, and the circulating-load band 400--600\%.

\paragraph{Operating point and scenarios.} The nominal feed is the centre of
the TD-MPC's training range: 1\,250~m$^3$/h of slurry, ore density
2.60~t/m$^3$ and 41.5\% solids by volume. The nominal recipe is 9 cyclones,
11~psi, a density setpoint of 1.697~t/m$^3$, 50~t/h of mill water and 13~rpm,
which gives a sump level of 75\% and a P80 of 188~$\mu$m. Disturbances act on
the feed flow ($\pm$2--8\% in the development range), the ore density
($\pm$0.03--0.10~t/m$^3$) and the solids content ($\pm$0.5--1.5 percentage
points), so that the development range is the TD-MPC's training range. They
start after 5--20 minutes, as steps, ramps over 10--30 minutes or sines with
periods of 20--60 minutes. The nominal scenarios step the P80 target by
2.5--5\% and back. Beyond the development range the amplitudes are 1.25 to 2
times the half-widths of the training range (provisional). One scenario per
cell gives 14 development, 14 held-out and 12 beyond-range scenarios for
training.

\paragraph{Score.} The score per scenario is
\begin{equation}
  J_s = \mathrm{IAE}_{\mathrm{P80},s} + 0.1\,(B_s + C_s) + V_s
        + 50\,\mathrm{TV}_s + 10^3\,E_s ,
  \label{eq:mill-score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{IAE}_{\mathrm{P80},s}$ is the integrated absolute P80 error in
$\mu$m$\cdot$h, $B_s$ and $C_s$ the seconds with the sump level and the
circulating load outside their bands, $V_s$ the seconds outside the safety
bounds of the sump level (including the rest of the episode after the
simulator stops), $\mathrm{TV}_s$ the total variation of the setpoints, each
normalised by its range, and $E_s$ the number of exceptions (provisional
weights). The recovery time to within 5\% of the P80 target and the mill power
are reported but not scored.

\paragraph{Training and comparators.} The trainer is the loop of
Section~\ref{sec:training-loop} with three candidates per generation and the
guard schedule, ported to the simulator. A generation takes about 45 minutes
of simulation. Besides the fixed recipe, a decentralised PI supervisor serves
as a simple baseline: it trims the density setpoint from the P80 error and the
pressure setpoint from the sump level. The TD-MPC was trained by Weir on the
same simulator setting with the feed held constant over each episode within
the development range; it learns a latent model of the circuit and plans the
same five setpoints over a 40-minute horizon. Its trained model is to be
evaluated on the same batteries.
