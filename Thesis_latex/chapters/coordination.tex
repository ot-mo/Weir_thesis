\chapter{Setpoint coordination}
\label{ch:coord}
The toy examples in Chapter~\ref{ch:toy} asked the supervisor to detect
faults. The research questions instead ask how well a generated supervisor
\emph{coordinates setpoints} under disturbances compared with a model-based
predictive controller, inside and beyond the development range. This chapter
rehearses that comparison on the quadruple-tank process before the industrial
study. The runs reported here are \emph{exploratory}: the training loop was
changed between them, and the validation batteries were looked at after each
run. They led to the pre-registered protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, whose confirmatory runs are reported separately.
Unless stated otherwise, every score in this chapter is evaluated with the
current test bed on the same three batteries, so the numbers are comparable;
lower is better.

\section{Test bed}
\label{sec:coord-testbed}
\paragraph{Process and objectives.} The plant is the quadruple-tank process
of Section~\ref{sec:four}. Two cross-paired PI loops hold the levels $h_1$
and $h_2$ of the lower tanks at their setpoints. Table~\ref{tab:coord-analog}
maps the grinding-circuit objectives onto the process. The product is the
production rate $Q$, the summed outflow of the two lower tanks, which is
16.4~L/s at the nominal setpoints $h_1 = 0.30$~m and $h_2 = 0.35$~m. The
level $h_2$ must stay inside the band 0.25--0.45~m, and the upper levels
$h_3$ and $h_4$ must stay below 0.75~m. The supervisor moves only the two
setpoints, within 0.05--0.48~m. Two setpoints and one production target
leave one degree of freedom, which the supervisor must use to keep the
constraints satisfied as disturbances move the operating point.

\begin{table}[tb]
  \centering
  \caption{The grinding-circuit objectives and their analogs on the
    quadruple-tank process.}
  \label{tab:coord-analog}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule
    Grinding circuit & Quadruple-tank analog \\
    \midrule
    P80 on target                  & production rate $Q$ on target \\
    Sump level stable              & $h_2$ inside a band \\
    Circulating load within limits & $h_3$ and $h_4$ below a limit \\
    Fresh feed flow variation      & draw-off at tank 1 or 2, extra inflow at tank 2 \\
    Ore density variation          & loss of pump gain $k_1$ or $k_2$ \\
    Percentage solids variation    & shift of valve split $\gamma_1$ or $\gamma_2$ \\
    Setpoint smoothness            & total variation of the setpoints \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{PI loops.} The leak test beds used $K_p = 40$~V/m and
$T_i = 133$~s on both loops. On this test bed those gains gave a maximum
sensitivity of $M_s = 2.98$ and about 40\% overshoot, while the loops of a
real plant can be assumed to be well tuned. The loops were therefore retuned
by a search over $K_p$ and $T_i$ for the lowest integrated error after a load
disturbance subject to $M_s \le 1.6$. The result, $K_p = 15$~V/m and
$T_i = 133$~s on the $h_1$ loop and $K_p = 40$~V/m and $T_i = 200$~s on the
$h_2$ loop, has $M_s = 1.56$. All runs in this chapter were \emph{trained}
with the original loops; their champions are re-scored with the retuned
ones. Section~\ref{sec:coord-results} discusses what this costs them.

\paragraph{Scenarios.} Each 1\,200~s episode starts in steady operation, as
a running plant would, and is integrated with the explicit Euler method at a
1~s step. A disturbance of one of three kinds (feed, pump or split, as in
Table~\ref{tab:coord-analog}), or a combination of two of them at three
quarters of the amplitude, starts between 150 and 300~s, as a step, a ramp
over 200--400~s or a sine with a period of 200--500~s. The development
amplitudes were calibrated on the steady-state model so that some setpoint
pair still meets the production target with every constraint satisfied,
while the upper part of each range breaks the fixed operating recipe. The
amplitudes beyond the development range start past that edge of
solvability, so some constraint violation there cannot be avoided. Two
further nominal scenarios step the production target up or down by 5--10\%
and back after 400--550~s. With three scenarios per cell, the development
battery has 42 scenarios (12 cells of disturbance kind and pattern, plus the
nominal scenarios). The held-out battery has 42 scenarios of the same types
drawn with another seed, and the battery beyond the development range has 36.
The roles of the batteries are described in Section~\ref{sec:batteries}.

\paragraph{Supervisor interface.} The supervisor is called every 10~s as
\begin{center}
\texttt{supervise(telemetry\_window, active\_setpoints, objectives)}
\end{center}
and returns a diagnosis and the two new setpoints; there are no anomaly
flags. The window holds the last 600 one-second samples of the four levels,
both pump voltages, the production rate, and the setpoints and production
target that were active at each sample, as a distributed control system logs
them. The objectives are the production target, the band, the upper limit and
the setpoint limits. The supervisor is stateless, as before, so the window is
its only memory. An earlier 50~s window was too short for this: the
supervisor could not tell a sine disturbance from a ramp or a step, and it
could not see its own setpoint changes or a target change settling (about
70~s to within 2\% with the original loops). The first generated supervisors
read that transient as a disturbance and chattered their setpoints. Every
controller, generated or not, passes through the safety wrapper of
Section~\ref{sec:wrapper}, with a rate limit of 0.12~m per call. When the limit was set, the largest
step any controller had taken on the three batteries was 0.106~m (the
untuned MPC), so the limit is a safety net, not a performance limit.

\paragraph{Score.} For a scenario $s$,
\begin{equation}
  J_s = \mathrm{IAE}_{Q,s} + 2\,(B_s + U_s) + 10\,V_s + 100\,\mathrm{TV}_s
        + 10^3\,E_s ,
  \label{eq:coord-score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{IAE}_{Q,s}$ is the integrated absolute production error in
litres, $B_s$ and $U_s$ the number of seconds with $h_2$ outside its band and
with an upper level above its limit, $V_s$ the number of seconds outside the
safety bounds of the lower levels, $\mathrm{TV}_s$ the total variation of the
two setpoints in metres and $E_s$ the number of exceptions and timeouts. One
litre off target costs as much as half a second of constraint violation or a
centimetre of setpoint travel. The weights were chosen without the plant's
operators and are frozen in the protocol. The recovery time, the time until
production settles within 2\% of the target after an event for good, is
reported but not scored. No controller in this chapter violated the safety
bounds, raised an exception or triggered the safety wrapper in nominal
conditions.

\section{Baselines}
\label{sec:coord-baselines}
\paragraph{Fixed recipe.} The seed supervisor keeps a fixed operating
recipe: when the production target changes, it scales both setpoints with
the nominal square-root relation $Q \propto \sqrt{h}$, and it ignores
disturbances. This is what the PI loops achieve on their own.

\paragraph{Model predictive controller.} The model-based predictive baseline
simulates candidate setpoint pairs over a prediction horizon with an
internal model of the plant and the PI loops, and applies the pair with the
lowest predicted cost. The candidates are a coarse $9 \times 9$ grid of moves
around the current setpoints and then a finer $5 \times 5$ grid around the
best one; each pair is held over the horizon. The disturbance is estimated
online as an additive inflow per tank: after each decision, the difference
between the measured and the predicted state is folded into the estimate
with a gain, the bias update common in industrial MPC \cite{qin2003}. Unlike
the generated supervisors, the MPC is stateful. It stands in for the
model-based predictive benchmark of the research questions; it is not the
TD-MPC controller used at Weir. Three variants are evaluated.
\begin{description}
\item[Untuned MPC] The original controller, written for this thesis before
  any comparison: a 300~s horizon, a quadratic cost on the relative
  production error, the size of band and upper-limit violations, pump
  voltages outside 1.5--11.5~V and setpoint moves, and estimator gain 0.5.
\item[Tuned MPC] The LLM's supervisor is optimised directly against the
  score on the development battery; the best champion comes from a lineage
  of about 40 candidates. To give the MPC a comparable chance, 40
  configurations were drawn by a seeded random search on the same battery
  (configuration 0 being the untuned MPC), and the best was kept. The
  search covered the cost form, the horizon, the estimator gain and the
  cost weights. The winner uses a cost made of the score's own terms over the
  horizon (absolute production error, seconds within a margin of 0.0128~m of
  the constraints, and the score's price on setpoint travel multiplied by
  3.78), a pump-headroom weight of 27.4, a 200~s horizon and estimator gain
  0.774. Its development score is 158.8, against 202.0 for the untuned MPC.
  The held-out and beyond batteries were not used in the tuning. This is the
  primary comparator of the protocol.
\item[Tuned MPC with known disturbance] The tuned MPC given the true current
  plant parameters and disturbance at every decision, held constant over the
  horizon. It is a reference, not a lower bound on the score: its model and
  cost still differ from the score, and it loses to the tuned MPC on 19 of
  the 120 validation scenarios.
\end{description}

\section{Changes to the training loop}
\label{sec:coord-loop}
The coordination runs used the training loop of
Section~\ref{sec:training-loop}, with the score \eqref{eq:coord-score}, the
security sandbox, deepseek-flash and three candidates per generation. Four
parts of the loop described there were added during these runs, each after a
run that stalled:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Regression guard by disturbance type, with a schedule.} The
  per-scenario guard of the leak test beds rejected almost every candidate.
  In the first 600~s run, every candidate that beat the seed (the best scored
  258 against 533 with the original loops) was rejected, so the prompt kept
  showing only the seed. With the guard by disturbance type and a schedule
  that is loose in the first generations, a candidate that is much better
  overall but worse on one scenario can be promoted early, and its weak
  scenario then appears in the next prompt's decision traces.
\item \emph{Failure traces of rejected candidates.} The prompt also shows,
  for up to two non-promoted candidates of the previous generation, the
  decision trace of the scenario each lost most on.
\item \emph{One targeted change per candidate.} Without this rule,
  candidates rewrote the supervisor, keeping 16--65\% of the champion's lines,
  and each fixed one scenario while breaking others. With it they kept
  83--99\%.
\item \emph{A measured record instead of unverified lessons.} The lessons are
  written by the LLM before its candidate is scored. In one run, all nine
  candidates made the same change (averaging the load estimate), which damped
  the response to sine disturbances and broke the response to steps, and the
  lessons saved from them recommended that change again. Lessons are now kept
  only from promoted candidates, and every other candidate is recorded as a
  measured line in the prompt.
\end{itemize}

The numbers in the rest of this paragraph were measured during the runs,
with the original PI loops. The guard schedule gave the first promotion with

The next nine generations at low reasoning effort, the rest of that run and
two continuations from its champion, promoted nothing, although the measured

estimators that accounted for oscillating disturbances (the best scored
241.5). The same loop at high reasoning effort, seeded with that champion
and its record of nine measured attempts, promoted a candidate in each of
four generations: 230.3, 227.4, 212.4 and 206.3. The four changes were, in
order, a pump-gain estimate corrected for the flow stored in the upper tanks,
a feedback trim of the $h_1$ setpoint from the measured production error, a
predictive version of that trim, and a shorter hold after the supervisor's
own setpoint changes. A request at high effort produced 53\,000--131\,000
output tokens (84\,000 on average) from a prompt of 18\,000--25\,000 tokens,

the looser guard score 352.0, 291.5 and 417.3 on the development battery.
This is a first indication, for contribution~C3, that independent runs of the
same setup can end far apart.

\label{sec:coord-stress}

The tuned MPC is the most fragile of the four controllers. Measurement noise
of 3~mm more than doubles its score, which puts it behind the generated
supervisor (421.0 against 278.1), and it degrades most under plant mismatch
and when $h_3$ reads NaN, where the safety wrapper holds every controller's
setpoints for the rest of the episode. Its tuning made it sharper on the
nominal model at the cost of robustness. The generated supervisor is almost
unaffected by noise, presumably because it estimates the disturbance from
averages and slopes over the 600~s window, and it is the best of the four when $h_3$ fails. The
analyser delay and the frozen production measurement do not affect the MPCs,
which predict production from the levels instead of reading it, and change the generated
supervisor by 3--4\%. Under plant mismatch every controller degrades
strongly, the fixed recipe least in relative terms; in absolute terms the
generated supervisor and the tuned MPC remain close. These conditions show
that ranking the controllers on the nominal test bed alone is incomplete.

\section{The generated supervisor}
\label{sec:coord-champion}
The best supervisor is a single Python function of 261 lines. It works as a
steady-state optimiser with a feedback trim, a structure that the LLM arrived
at by itself from the seed's fixed recipe:
\begin{enumerate}
\item It estimates the extra inflow to each lower tank from a mass balance:
  the measured outflow minus the inflow that the upper tanks' outflows imply
  through the nominal valve splits.
\item It estimates each pump's gain from the flow into its upper tank,
  including the measured rate of change of the upper level, so that flow
  stored in an upper tank during an oscillation is not mistaken for a gain
  loss. The estimate is limited to 50--100\% of the nominal gain.
\item It divides the production target between the two lower tanks by
  trying 61 splits. For each it computes, from the steady-state model with
  the estimated disturbance, the required pump flows, the upper levels and
  the pump voltages, and it penalises upper levels within 0.05~m of the limit,
  $h_2$ outside its band, saturated pumps and the size of the setpoint move.
  The resulting setpoints move at most 0.06~m per decision.
\item It trims the $h_1$ setpoint with the production error predicted 80~s
  ahead from the error's slope over the last 90~s. The prediction may
  reinforce the measured error but never change its sign, so steps and target
  changes can only be corrected faster, and the trim is limited by the
  remaining headroom of tank~3.
\item It holds its setpoints for 25~s after its own last setpoint change,
  unless the production target changed since, so that it does not react to
  its own transient.
\end{enumerate}
The code added by the promoted candidates is commented with the reasoning
behind it, in one place including the measured effect of an earlier
variant. The lessons kept from the four promoted candidates are statements
about the process, for example that ``a setpoint change needs 1.5--2 minutes
to reach the product, so a proportional error trim corrects each swing after
its trough''. Steps 4 and 5 are exactly the constants that describe the
transient of the original PI loops. That they are visible and commented is
what made it possible to point to them as the likely cause of the loss with
the retuned loops; in an MPC the same knowledge sits in the internal model,
which was updated with the loops.

\section{Status of the confirmatory comparison}
\label{sec:coord-status}

answer $H_{0,1}$ or $H_{0,2}$. The pre-registered protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:protocol} fixes all of them and runs eight independent runs
of six generations each at high reasoning effort, from the fixed recipe and
with the retuned loops, against the tuned MPC on the sealed test battery.
As of this draft the protocol is registered (version~2) and no protocol run
has been made.
