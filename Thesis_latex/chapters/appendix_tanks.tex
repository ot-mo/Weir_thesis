\label{app:tanks}

% the grinding circuit). The main text keeps only Section sec:dev-lessons of

Before the grinding circuit was available, the method was built on four tank
test beds of increasing difficulty, with DeepSeek's \texttt{deepseek-flash}.
The first three ask the supervisor to detect leaks; the fourth rehearses
setpoint coordination against an MPC. Their batteries had a \emph{held-out}
battery that was shown to the LLM only as a gap and never used for promotion.
Section~\ref{sec:dev-lessons} summarises what they taught; this appendix gives
the evidence.

\section{Lessons, with their evidence}
\subsection{The test beds}
\label{sec:tank-testbeds}
% Setup in two sentences each; details in Appendix app:tanks.
\paragraph{Leak detection.} Three processes with leaks as the fault: a
single tank, a two-tank cascade and the quadruple-tank process
\cite{johansson2000} from the PC-Gym benchmark suite \cite{bloor2024pcgym}.
The supervisor raises one anomaly flag per loop and may adjust the setpoints;
the score (Appendix~\ref{app:tanks}) charges tracking error, safety
violations, missed anomalies and false positives.
Model-based fault detection compares measured behaviour with what a model
predicts for the fault-free case and alarms when the residual is large enough
\cite{isermann2006}; the trade-off between missed detections, false alarms and
delay is reflected in the score. On all three processes the generated
supervisors reduced the development score by 72--96\% against PID control
alone (Table~\ref{tab:summary}).

\paragraph{Setpoint coordination.}

The coordination test bed is the quadruple-tank process with two
cross-paired PI loops (Table~\ref{tab:coord-analog}). The product is the
production rate $Q$, the summed outflow of the lower tanks; the level $h_2$
must stay inside a band and the upper levels below a limit. Two setpoints and
one production target leave one degree of freedom. Disturbances on the feed,
a pump gain or a valve split come as steps, ramps or sines, with 42
development, 42 held-out and 36 beyond-range scenarios of 1\,200~s. The
supervisor is called every 10~s on a 600~s window, and the score is
\begin{equation}
  J_s = \mathrm{IAE}_{Q,s} + 2\,(B_s + U_s) + 10\,V_s + 100\,\mathrm{TV}_s
        + 10^3\,E_s ,
  \label{eq:coord-score}
\end{equation}
% IAE_Q: production error (L); B, U: seconds with h2 outside its band / an
% upper level above its limit; V: seconds outside safety bounds; TV: setpoint
% total variation (m); E: exceptions and timeouts.

\subsection{Specification gaming and the regression guard}
\label{sec:tank-gaming}
% Lesson 1 (two-tank cascade).
The two-tank cascade exposed specification gaming: a candidate
improved the average score by flagging anomalies almost constantly. A false
positive cost the same in the fault-free scenario as anywhere else, and the
loss there was outweighed by fewer missed anomalies elsewhere. Other
candidates improved the average while making one scenario much worse; 10 of
58 trials were of this kind. This led to the asymmetric false-positive cost
and to the regression guard (Section~\ref{sec:promotion}).

\subsection{Check the test bed before the search}
\label{sec:tank-testbed-check}
% Lesson 2 (quadruple tank, fault detection).
The first training runs on the quadruple-tank process stalled at
$J \approx 36\,300$. The cause was three defects in the test bed rather than
a limit of the search: faults injected through PC-Gym's environment wrapper
were never applied; a decision interval equal to the window length put a
floor on the score; and the diagonal loop pairing ($\lambda = -0.07$) made the
severe tank-1 scenario impossible for any setpoint policy. At the plateau the
champion was within about 7\% of the best score any supervisor could reach on
the flawed test bed. A perfect-detection oracle, which sets each flag to the
true fault state, exposed the plateau; computing such a bound before
interpreting a plateau would have revealed the problem much earlier. After
the corrections, the best supervisors matched the oracle
(Table~\ref{tab:four}).

\subsection{Reasoning is needed; low effort is enough for detection}
\label{sec:tank-reasoning}
% Lesson 3: the only model ablation in the thesis. Keep.
The same search was run from the seed supervisor with three reasoning
settings (Figure~\ref{fig:reasoning} and Table~\ref{tab:reasoning}). With
high and low reasoning the search matched the oracle on both batteries within
three and two generations, respectively, and the held-out scores followed the
development scores. Without reasoning the search improved quickly at first
but stalled at 11\,837 from the third generation on, about 4.5 times the
oracle score. Three weaknesses stood out without reasoning: it missed 49--83
samples in each scenario with a mild leak, against 4--12 with reasoning; its lessons contained
incorrect physics, for example that a leak lowers the other loop's effort
through a shared outflow path that does not exist; and three of twelve
candidates in generations 4--6 called a built-in function that the sandbox
does not provide, after the prompt's list of allowed functions ended in
``etc.''. The list is now generated from the sandbox itself. With high
reasoning, 8 of 15 requests returned an empty response that was still
billed.

\subsection{What the search needs}
\label{sec:tank-search}
% Lesson 4: exactly what the mill trainer inherited. One sentence per change;
% the full run table is in Appendix app:tanks (tab:coord-runs).
The coordination runs used the training loop of
Section~\ref{sec:training-loop} with three candidates per generation. Four
parts of that loop were added during these runs, each after a run that
stalled (all runs in Table~\ref{tab:coord-runs}, Appendix~\ref{app:tanks}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Regression guard by disturbance type, with a schedule.} The
  per-scenario guard of the leak test beds rejected every candidate that beat
  the seed in the first 600~s run (the best scored 258 against 533), so the
  prompt kept showing only the seed.
\item \emph{Failure traces of rejected candidates}, so that the LLM sees why a
  change failed.
\item \emph{One targeted change per candidate.} Without this rule,
  candidates rewrote the supervisor, keeping 16--65\% of the champion's lines,
  and each fixed one scenario while breaking others. With it they kept
  83--99\%.
\item \emph{A measured record instead of unverified lessons.} In one run all
  nine candidates made the same change, which failed, and the lessons saved
  from them recommended it again.
\end{itemize}
The guard schedule gave the first promotion with the 600~s window. Nine
further generations at low effort promoted nothing; the same loop at high
effort, seeded with that champion and its record, promoted in each of four
generations, so the effects of effort and record are not separated. Repeated
runs of the same setup ended far apart (development 292--417), a first sign
for C3. All coordination runs cost about US\$4.1.

\subsection{A comparator is only as good as its tuning}
\label{sec:tank-comparator}
% Lesson 5. Keep tab:coord-main. tab:coord-metrics and tab:coord-kind dropped.
The model-based comparator was an MPC written for this thesis: it searches
a grid of setpoint pairs with an internal model of the plant and the PI loops,
and estimates the disturbance with the bias update common in industrial MPC
\cite{qin2003}. It was evaluated untuned, tuned by a random search of 40
configurations on the development battery (a budget comparable to the LLM's
candidates), and with the true disturbance as a reference. After the runs the
PI loops were retuned to $M_s \le 1.6$; the champions were not retrained.

Table~\ref{tab:coord-main} compares the best generated supervisor, the
champion of the high-effort run, with the baselines. It reduces the fixed
recipe's development score by 47\%, but it is 67\% worse than the tuned MPC
(265.4 against 158.8). With the original PI loops and against the untuned
MPC, the same champion had come within 1.3\% (206.3 against 203.7). Two
things changed after the runs. First, the MPC was tuned with a budget
comparable to the LLM's, which lowered its development score by 21\%. Second,
the PI loops were retuned. The MPC's internal model includes the loops, so it
adapts to new gains, but the generated supervisor encodes the old regulatory
dynamics as constants (hold times, prediction horizons of its trim, settling
assumptions), and its development score rose by 59 points. Which constants
cause the loss was not investigated further.
The champion's deficit is production error (230.5 against 119.7~L per
scenario for the tuned MPC), and it recovers about twice as slowly. Beyond
the range both degrade by a factor of about 4.6--4.8, the generated supervisor
by giving up the constraints to hold production.

\subsection{The generated coordination supervisor}
\label{sec:coord-champion}
% Short version; the five-step walkthrough is dropped unless pages allow.
The best coordination supervisor is a single 261-line function that the LLM
turned from the fixed recipe into a steady-state optimiser: a disturbance
estimator from mass balances, a search over 61 splits of the production target
against a cost, and a predictive feedback trim, an MPC-like structure with
every part visible. Its hold time and trim horizon are the constants that
describe the old loops' transient, which is how the loss with the retuned
loops could be pointed to in the code.

\section{Test-bed details}

\subsection{Fault-detection interface and score}
On the leak test beds the supervisor is
\begin{center}
\texttt{supervise(telemetry\_window, active\_setpoints, nominal\_targets)}.
\end{center}
The telemetry window holds, for each controlled tank
and each sample, the level, the output of the loop regulating it (pump
effort) and the control error. The function returns a free-text diagnosis,
one adjusted setpoint per loop and one boolean anomaly flag per loop. The
returned flags are held until the next decision. An exception or timeout
raises both anomaly flags.

The scenarios are a fault-free run,
single leaks of different onset, size and duration, simultaneous leaks,
persistent severe leaks and, for the single- and two-tank processes, sensor
noise. The score is
\begin{equation}
  J_s = \mathrm{IAE}_s + 500\,V_s + 300\,M_s + c_{\mathrm{FP}}\,F_s
        + 10^4\,E_s + 200\,R_s ,
  \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{IAE}_s$ is the integrated absolute tracking error of all
loops, $V_s$ the number of samples outside the safety bounds, $M_s$ and $F_s$
the number of samples, summed over loops, in which a held anomaly flag
disagrees with the true fault state (missed anomalies and false positives),
$E_s$ the number of exceptions and timeouts, and $R_s$ the distance between
the final and the nominal setpoints for loops whose fault has cleared. A
false positive on a loop that never faults in the scenario costs
$c_{\mathrm{FP}} = 800$ (250 on the quadruple-tank process); otherwise
$c_{\mathrm{FP}} = 160$. Without this asymmetry, flagging constantly was a
cheap way to avoid missed anomalies. For the quadruple-tank process an
\emph{oracle} is also evaluated. It sets each flag to the true fault state at
every decision and keeps the nominal setpoints, which is the best detection
possible at the given decision interval.

The regression guard on the leak test beds is per scenario: no single
scenario may get worse than the champion by more than 5 points (the
fault-free scenario) or the larger of 100 points and 15\% (fault scenarios).

\subsection{Single tank}
One tank with a pump controlled by a PID loop
($K_p = 2$, $K_i = 0.5$, $K_d = 0.1$) at a sample time of 0.1~s, and an
unmeasured leak as the fault. The supervisor decides every 3~s on the last
30 samples of a 30~s episode. The development battery has eight scenarios:
fault-free, a standard leak, an early severe leak, a late mild leak, two
leaks that start and stop, and a fault-free and a leaking scenario with
sensor noise. Of 62 trials, 16 were promoted. The final supervisor
reduced the development score by 76\% and the held-out score by 66\%
compared with PID alone. Early supervisors lowered the setpoint during a fault
and never raised it again, which led to the restoration term $R_s$ in
\eqref{eq:score}.

\subsection{Two-tank cascade}
Tank~1 drains by gravity into tank~2, and each tank has
its own pump and PID loop with the same gains as above. A leak in tank~1
alone moves the level of tank~2 by more than 2~m, so the supervisor must tell
propagated disturbances from genuine faults. Of 58 trials, 7 were promoted and
10 were rejected by the regression guard. The final supervisor reduced the
development score by 72\% and the held-out score by 61\%.

\subsection{Quadruple-tank process}
The quadruple-tank process \cite{johansson2000} from the
PC-Gym benchmark suite \cite{bloor2024pcgym} has four tanks and two pumps.
Each pump feeds one lower tank directly and, through a split valve, the upper
tank above the other lower tank. With the split fractions
$\gamma_1 = \gamma_2 = 0.2$, most of each pump's flow takes the delayed path,
which gives a non-minimum-phase process. Only the two lower levels are
measured. The model is integrated with the explicit Euler method at a 1~s
step over 600~s episodes. A leak multiplies the outlet area of a lower tank
by 1.8--4. There are six development and six held-out scenarios, and the
supervisor decides every 10~s on the last 50~s of telemetry. After re-pairing
the loops off-diagonally, they are PI controllers with $K_p = 40$ and
$K_i = 0.3$.

\section{The four-tank protocol (version 2)}
\label{app:four-tank-protocol}
During the exploratory coordination runs, the training loop was redesigned
about a dozen times while the development, held-out and beyond results were
being looked at, and the best champion came out of a hand-steered chain of
runs. Such a result cannot test a hypothesis. Before any confirmatory run,
a protocol was therefore registered in the repository. It fixes, in one
commit, the test bed, the score weights, the batteries, the method, the
number of runs, the comparator and the statistics. Any change creates a new
version and restarts the runs; runs under an earlier version are reported,
not discarded. Version~2, the current one, was registered after the PI loops
of the test bed were retuned and the MPC re-tuned accordingly, before any
protocol run and before any access to the sealed battery.

\paragraph{Runs and policies.} $N = 8$ independent runs of six generations
each, all from the fixed recipe, with \texttt{deepseek-flash} at high
reasoning effort and three candidates per generation. A run that fails for
technical reasons is resumed under the same name, never replaced or dropped.
The policy of each run is its final champion; it is not chosen by any other
score.

\paragraph{Comparator.} The primary comparator is the tuned MPC with frozen
parameters; the untuned MPC is secondary, and the fixed recipe and the tuned
MPC with known disturbance are references.

\paragraph{Sealed test battery.} 183 scenarios with their own seed: 42 in the
development range (including nominal target changes), 36 each at 1.25, 1.5
and 2 times a development draw, 24 with sine periods outside the development
range (100--180~s and 550--800~s), and 9 with feed, pump and split
disturbances together. The battery is evaluated once, after all runs are
complete.

\paragraph{Analyses.} All intervals come from a hierarchical bootstrap
($B = 10\,000$) that resamples policies and then scenarios.
\begin{itemize}
\item $H_{0,1}$: the mean paired difference in score, LLM minus tuned MPC,
  over the 8 policies and the 42 development-range test scenarios. $H_{0,1}$
  is rejected if the 95\% interval excludes zero. Failing to reject does not
  establish parity: the two are called equivalent only if the 90\% interval
  lies within $\pm 10\%$ of the MPC's mean score (two one-sided tests).
\item $H_{0,2}$: the slope of the mean $\log(1 + J_s)$ against the amplitude
  level (1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2), LLM minus MPC. $H_{0,2}$ is rejected if the
  95\% interval of the slope difference excludes zero.
\item C3: the median, interquartile range and range of the per-policy mean
  scores, the between-policy standard deviation with a chi-square
  confidence interval, and the number of runs without any promotion.
\end{itemize}
Secondary analyses are the per-term differences with Holm-corrected
bootstrap $p$-values, the score ratios on the unseen periods and
combinations, and results per disturbance type and pattern. Stress tests
with noise, plant mismatch, analyser delay and telemetry faults, and two
ablations (a prompt without the plant equations, and the best of 18 one-shot
samples without the feedback loop) are planned as exploratory.
